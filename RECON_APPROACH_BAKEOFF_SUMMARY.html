Recon architecture bake-off

PO ↔ audit report ↔ invoice reconciliation · tested on real Bata documents (Thane 8255, Mangalore 9621) · Nov 2026

Recommendation: build C (hybrid).

All three designs calculate the payable correctly. They differ in what happens after the first run: whether a reviewer's decisions survive, whether a past verdict can be replayed, and how many systems must stay up. C was the only one to pass every test.

Key takeaways

  • Storage format isn't the issue. Relational and JSON are close on speed and size. What matters is keeping human decisions in a permanent, append-only log.
  • The vocabulary-learning loop works as a safety guard. It auto-handled only 1 of 6 new client labels; the rest correctly went to a person. With the guard off, one misleading column header silently over-paid ₹18,660.
  • Fix the dictionary first. Five terms the schemas rely on are missing from it, so the Thane audit report is rejected as written.
  • One business decision is needed. Should the typed audit table or the signed site sheet set the payable? They give ₹6,06,740.50 vs ₹6,08,143.50.

Not tested yet

  • Real Postgres: ran on SQLite. A quick re-run is worth it to confirm storage sizes.
  • A real graph database: not needed, because B's problems are in the design, not the engine.
  • AI extraction: it doesn't affect this choice; it should be tested separately, with approval to use client documents.
